When buying the motor it is important to calculate how much power you need based on how long your system is, how many 90 deg turns it has and how high: NFT usually uses 1-2liters/per minute per channel and liter/min = 15.85 GPH, so with 4 channels getting a 1.5L/min each, you would need a pump that has around 95.1 GPH. Something else that will contribute to this number is head height, for example, a 90 deg elbow will add about 1-2ft per turn to the head height, and every 10ft of 1/2" tubing will add about 2 ft of head height, but since my stsem used 4" tubes the ammount of head height added is much lower, lastly we add the height difference between the verticle height of the reservoir to the highest point of the water. After making your calculations you will need to find a pump that has the GPH you calculated, at the head height you calculated, sicne pumps loose much of their power as head height increases.
When deciding upon a water reservoir our calculations for the size could be based on the voulme of water all of our tubing will use at a time or based on the plant uptake for all the plants projected to be on our system, for example if we plan to only plant lettuce and we find that each plant requires 2 L, we can multiply by the number of plants we want, say 40 giving us 2 L x 40 = 80 (our minumum), to this we factor a 20 - 30% extra as a buffer so 80 x 1.3 = 104 L (around 27 gallon reservoir). The other method would be to use channel size, were the channel volume would = Channel Width x film Depth x Length x Number of channels. The actual reservoir should be asbout 5 - 10x the channel volume
Ensure that all of your materials are built out of food grade materials, these are usually rated codes: 1 , 2, 4, and 5

Project Overview

Purpose

The purpose of this project was to utilize hydroponics as a means to grow crops vertically in a semi-controlled enviroment, thereby allowing the growth of crops for a more extensive period of time, utilizing both less space and allowing for more automation with a budget of $500.

In a broader spectrum, the issue that my system aims to solve is allowing people who aren't as knowledable in growing crops to start growing their own food with a system that allows for monitoring in ways that aren't traditional to soil growing, like measuring PH, and EC, but with a striaght forward approach. Additionally, my system aims to solve upon an issue known quite commonly among New York residents which is the ever changing weather.

Why choose NFT

The reason I chose the NFT is because of it's ease to integrate as a wall build. With other systems like kratsky, deep water culture, aeroponics or even ebb and flow, the plants are placed directly on the water reservoir, or are simply more efficient if done horizontally.

This system was designed to feed 1 person in it's current form but could be expanded to feed more.


A major considertaiong that must be made before starting to build this system or any NFT system at all is the risk of system failure. To elaborate, NFT systems require water pumps, air pumps, and a great amount of interconnected parts, this means a high chance of leaks which could lower the water efficiency of the farm, and motor failures which could destroy entire harvests. However, there are ways to mitigate the chances of this happening and to even safe the harvest if something does happen and get back to buisness.

The main advantages of NFT, are it's water efficiency, since a system without leaks can keep recycling most of it's water, and the availability of oxygen to the plant roots allowing for less chances of root rot ( a very common plant desease caused by lack of oxygen to the roots).


Due to the water efficiency of this system and it's ability to keep producing crops for a longer period of time out of the year (depending on how it is set up) it is a very sustainable choice so long as the neccessary maintence is offered.

Project analysis

While building this system I encountered a challenge because the water would start leaking through the edges between the 4” tubes and their caps, in order to solve this problem I simply glued the caps to the 4” tubes. So I added rubber tubes in between the connected sections of tubing, and used a barbed connector which prevents water from leaking in those areas, so when I need to do maintenance, I simply remove the rubber tubes to gain access.
Another challenge I encountered was the fact that due to the location of my system, some parts didn't get as much light. Some ways I thought of for dealing with this issue were to place plants that need less light in those areas, and also to add reflective material to the 7ft wall behind the tubes to get more light to bounce back into those darker areas.
The water pump I have is 800 GPH, which is great due to its cheap price, however it started to overflow my system with water, in order to solve this, I bought a flow control valve and placed it where the water exited the pump, thus reducing the flow of water.
A broken water pump can immediately spill disaster upon an NFT system, which is why it is good practice to buy a second water pump that can be used in case the other one fails.

Conclusion

Though I have yet to test out the system with plants because mine are still germinating, I am very confident that it will work, and have done testing running it for hours at a time to check for water loss with much success. While the location I chose doesn't allow for as much light as would be ideal, my workaround should allow for the system to still operate nicely. In hindsight, the tubes I bought for my system weren't the ideal shape, when it comes to NFT it is best to work with flat bottomed media so that the nutrient film is nice and leveled the width of the media.
