Project Overview

Have you ever felt as though you have tried every possible application or watched every on YouTube, trying to find a way to maximize your efficiency? Well, I have, and chances are, so have you. This is where Taskfighter comes in; this Android application is meant to be used as a planner-style tool in which users can move all their tasks and events to understand better what their weeks will look like.

But apps like this are all too common. There is notion, Google tasks, Todoist, even google calendar and many more, so how will Taskfighter be unique? Well, Taskfighter will come with a system of rewards in a both online and offline environment that will make users want to keep completing their tasks to maintain their streak.

This idea comes from a Ted Talk by the creator of Duolingo, Luis von Ahn, who embarked on a journey to create a teaching app that would be as addictive as any social media. My goal for this app is to push users who have trouble completing their daily tasks to a point where they see long-term productivity improvements. This app will also include a "mortal combat" style social mode in which people will earn coins from challenges and be able to purchase items with said coins to fight against friends or even with friends, aside from being able to by apparels.

Competition

There are other apps out there that take on the theme of gamified todo-list app, these include:

Tiimo

Carrot


The way in which Task Fighter stands out from these is that it builds upon their shortcomings, for example: Tiimo's calendar view only allows for users to check days, which makes it harder to navigate to tasks that are longer term, it also only works on internet meaning that users will only be to create tasks when they have good internet. Carrot is Apple only which is an issue for any android users. Task fighter will use local and cloud storage for taks meaning that it will be able to work offline, it's calendar view can be switch from day, week, month, and year making it easier to view future tasks. And it will work on both Apple and Android by avoiding the use of either Kotlin or Swift.

conflict analysis

When a game is designed in which the user has the power to choose how much money they win, the purpose of the game is lost. When it comes to taks fighter it is easy to see how a person could create a task assign it a deficulty of hard, and a long time and simply obtain the reward. But in order to mitigate this, the system doesn't directly reward users based on completing the tasks. Once a task has been activated, no other tasks can be activated and a timer will start. Once the person is done with their task they can click done, and depending on how close their estimate got to their actual time, the more money they get(on top of the difficulty and duration rewards), this will not only decrease the amount of cheating, but also ecourage people to track their time on tasks better.

Now, let's say a player has completed a few hard tasks and earned a lot of money in game, this would make the game boring since that player can now buy almost anything. Well, in order to keep the task completion entertaining, items can only be obtained through chests, which have high rarity items to keep the users engaged.

Conclusion

The idea to create a gamified todo-list app is very personal to me as I myself know the struggle with time management. Though I am yet to test this app with users, due to some issues I am currenly expiriencing with building out the multi-user capabilities, the system to prevent players from abusing the coin system, and ways to make people want to come back could make this app succesful in it's role of entrtaining people into managing their time. Looking to the future I want to implement AI to make it so that Taskfighter would help you organize your schedule in a way that you aren't putting too many tasks for one day or too little for another day, which could be done by gathering an individual's task completion data.
